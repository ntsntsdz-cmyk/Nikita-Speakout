Page 1: Core Grammar & Lexical Explanations
1. Transitive Verbs vs. Prepositional Errors
• Influence: Acts as a transitive verb directly affecting an object without prepositions.
  Correct: "Language influences our perception." (NOT "influences on")
• Impact: Takes "on" when used as a noun.
  Correct: "Language has an impact on culture."

2. Media and Language Prepositions

• Use in when stating the language medium of media, films, or literature.
  Correct: "I am watching a movie in English." (NOT "on English")
3. Verb Patterns: Try to do vs. Try doing
• Try + Infinitive (to do): Making an effort or attempt to accomplish something difficult.
  Example: "I am trying to learn ten new words a day."
• Try + Gerund (-ing): Experimenting with a method or action to see if it brings results.
  Example: "If you can't remember a word, try drawing a picture of it."

4. Expressing Contrast: In Spite Of

• In spite of + Noun / -ing: Followed directly by a noun phrase or gerund.
  Example: "In spite of the rain, we went out." / "In spite of feeling tired, she studied."
• In spite of the fact that + Clause: Required when followed by a subject and verb.
  Example: "In spite of the fact that language barriers exist, people communicate."
5. Adjective vs. Adverb: Good vs. Well
• Good (Adjective): Modifies nouns or follows linking verbs (be, sound, feel, look).
  Example: "That is a good presentation." / "It sounds good."
• Well (Adverb): Modifies verbs to describe how an action is performed.
  Example: "She speaks English very well."
